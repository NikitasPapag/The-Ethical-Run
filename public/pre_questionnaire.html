INFORMED CONSENT

Informed Consent for Study Participation

You are invited to take part in the user study "The Ethical Run". The study is conducted by Nikitas Papangelopoulos and overseen by Andreas Komninos at Computer Engineering and Informatics Department. We expect about 50 participants. Data collection is planned from 2026-11-23 to 2026-12-31. Key points:

  • Participation is voluntary. You may stop at any time without penalty or withdraw your consent
  • One session of the user study takes about 20 minutes
  • You will not receive compensation for participating
  • We collect demographic information (e.g. age and gender) for analysis
  • The study may involve collecting the following data: your input
  • Recordings and research data are handled confidentially. They will be fully anonymized, stored, analyzed, and published only in summarized form, so that it should no longer be possible to identify you directly.

The alternative is not to take part. If you have questions about the study, the consent process, or your rights as a participant, please contact Andreas Komninos. Please read the following information carefully and take the time you need before deciding.

1. Purpose and Goal of this Research

The purpose of this study is to evaluate the educational efficacy of a video game in teaching moral philosophy. The goal of this study is to investigate how effectively game-based dialogues help learners apply ethical reasoning frameworks, and to assess user engagement and perceived teaching effectiveness. Your participation supports this research. Results may be published in scientific papers, theses, or presented at academic conferences.

2. Study Participation

Your participation in this user study is voluntary. You may skip questions or tasks and stop at any time without penalty and without giving a reason. If you feel uncomfortable, you may stop immediately. The researchers may end your participation if this is necessary for organizational reasons, because of invalid trials, or for your safety. During participation, the house and safety rules of Computer Engineering and Informatics Department apply.

3. Study Procedure

If you agree to participate, the study will usually proceed as follows:

  1. Informed Consent
  2. Moral Framework Selection
  3. Demographic Profiling
  4. Pre-Game Evaluation
  5. Interactive Gameplay
  6. Post-Game Evaluation
  7. User Experience Feedback

If needed, the researchers can provide confirmation of participation after the study.

4. Risks and Benefits

Based on current knowledge, this user study does not involve risks beyond those of everyday activities. Temporary discomfort such as fatigue, mild irritation, or slight unease is possible, but overall unlikely. If you feel uncomfortable, please tell the researchers immediately or stop taking part. If an unexpected injury or health problem occurs, please stop immediately, inform the researchers, and seek medical help if needed. Despite technical and organizational safeguards, a loss of confidentiality or unauthorized access to data cannot be completely ruled out. You are unlikely to receive a direct personal benefit. However, your participation supports research in human-computer interaction.

5. Data Protection and Confidentiality

In this study, we collect directly identifying information, where necessary, and research data. Processing is based on your consent and carried out in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). You may request access to identifiable data, ask for incorrect information to be corrected, and - where legally possible - request restriction of processing or deletion. With your consent, we will collect your input. Study results may be published in scientific papers or other research reports. Directly identifying information will be kept only as long as necessary. It will then be deleted or separated from the research data. During analysis, only the researchers and authorized project staff will have access to the raw data. Directly identifying information will be stored separately and will not be published. The data will be anonymized as far as possible and published only in summarized form, so that it should no longer be possible to identify you directly. Because no contact details are collected, we cannot contact you afterwards about follow-up questions, future studies, or possible data protection incidents.
